Immediate safety help

If you’re in immediate danger contact 111

Call 111 and ask for police if the situation is urgent or is happening now.

This includes if:

  • someone is injured or in danger
  • a crime is in progress or has just happened
  • there is a serious risk to life or property.

Immediate safety lines

  • 111 Emergency Services
  • 111 TXT for hearing and speech difficulties
Crisis lines

Crisis lines

Crisis lines are support services you can contact any time, day or night, if you need urgent support after experiencing a crime or other traumatic events.

It may help to talk to someone if you feel overwhelmed, do not feel safe, or are worried about the safety of your whānau.

Immediate fraud help

Immediate fraud help

Fraud is very common in the modern world and is increasingly sophisticated. Modern fraud is designed by professionals to hook in people even if they are careful. If something feels wrong trust your feelings.

Here are some options about what to do when it happens and where to get help.

If you think you are being scammed, stop all contact immediately:

  • do not or share any more information
  • do not install software or programs
  • do not use any number or website given to you by the scammer.
